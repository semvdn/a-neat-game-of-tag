# Training experiments: selection, pace, and network size

## Decision

Separate held-out validation from breeding fitness and remove inactive retention branches. Keep the existing pace reward and Memory Discovery architecture. The reward and architecture trials did not establish a consistent improvement in mobile pursuit and evasion. In particular, higher tag counts sometimes came from nearly stationary Runners.

This is a selection-correctness fix supported by deterministic replay, not a claim that all gameplay metrics improved. The corrected selection produced more consistent Runner traversal, but also stronger evasion and some worse Chaser outcomes. The work does not yet establish exhibition-quality behavior over long unattended runs.

## What was wrong or uncertain

The original held-out pass assigned its winner `maxRawFitness + 1e-6`. The increment above the maximum was tiny, but the change to the winner's own score could be large. That changed reproduction and species allocation using evidence available to only a few candidates. It also changed which genome became the generation benchmark/archive champion. Validation now only ranks retained-champion candidates; every population genome keeps its common-panel fitness.

The worker also contained permanently disabled strict/cross-play gates and conditional branches for permanently enabled rules. Removing those paths makes the actual retention rule visible without introducing another training knob. Legacy descriptive flag fields remain in exports for compatibility.

Runner pace is capped, but still strong: at reward 15, six complete two-second windows can earn 90 points, versus a 60-point penalty for zero completion. That 150-point swing is large beside a 20-point tag/fall. Reducing this incentive is plausible, but the experiments show why changing it without retraining is unsafe: it can select inactivity instead of tactical evasion.

## Method

The new harness runs the real production worker, including NEAT reproduction, league evaluation, validation, archive, and retained champions. No surrogate evolutionary loop or scripted opponent replaces training. It uses the CPU fallback with 48 genomes per role and seeded initialization/reproduction. Source is pinned to `163f7ad`; experimental transformations are recorded and fail if their expected source changes.

- Screening: baseline, separate validation, pace reward 3, and Compact 12 for both roles; three initialization seeds (101, 202, 303), 12 generations each.
- Confirmation: baseline, separate validation, separate validation plus pace reward 8, and separate validation plus Compact 12 only for the Chaser; the same three seeds, 24 generations each.
- Total: 24 completed training runs, 432 generations and 241,920 counted population/league episode executions. Validation/retention episodes are additional. Baseline and separate-validation confirmation repeat their screening prefixes; these are not 24 independent replicates.
- Screening and confirmation compare early/middle/final retained checkpoints against one frozen opponent bank per report. The final comparison tests generation 24 against all 12 final opponent pairs, six start classes and three unused scenario seeds: 216 episodes per role/seed, 5,184 total.

Every candidate in a comparison receives the same opponents, starts and scenario seeds. Some opponents were previously encountered during training: only scenario seeds are held out. Architecture conditions change the complete preset, including mutation/recurrence settings, and consume different initialization random draws. They do not isolate node count alone.

The checked-in [numerical evidence](2026-09-08-results.json) contains the panel hashes, experiment provenance, aggregate trajectories and individual-seed results. The three principal comparisons contain 29,484 episode executions. Raw checkpoints and detailed per-start reports remain under `evaluations/`; commands in [the experiment guide](../TRAINING_EXPERIMENTS.md) reproduce them.

## Final common-panel results

Means across the three initialization seeds. Runner falls and landings cover both Runner slots. Counts are per episode; episodes may terminate early. Chaser tags come from testing that condition's Chaser against the common Runner bank; Runner statistics come from testing that condition's Runner against the common Chaser bank. These are not measurements of each condition's displayed pair playing itself.

| Condition | Chaser clean tags | Chaser falls | Runner catches conceded | Runner falls | Runner pace completion | Runner landings | Time within 200 px in Runner tests |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Original baseline | 0.600 | 0.182 | 0.796 | 2.838 | 61.3% | 4.520 | 22.6% |
| Separate validation | 0.616 | 0.307 | 0.130 | 2.849 | 88.1% | 8.715 | 11.8% |
| Separate validation + pace 8 | 0.640 | 0.125 | 1.432 | 1.593 | 49.7% | 2.247 | 37.0% |
| Separate validation + compact Chaser | 0.705 | 0.140 | 0.204 | 3.599 | 79.5% | 5.431 | 13.1% |

Separation alone preserves much more traversal and reduces catches conceded. It does not solve pursuit: Chaser falls increase, and Runner escape incidence rises from 0.483 to 0.793 per episode. This tradeoff is a reason to avoid claiming a universal gameplay win.

Pace 8 appears attractive in aggregate because it brings agents closer and reduces Runner falls. But seeds 101 and 303 average only 0.27 and 0.12 Runner landings per episode, versus 7.84 and 8.53 with separated validation at pace 15. Their pace completion is 26% and 41%. Seed 202 remains mobile at 82% and 6.35 landings. Two nearly non-traversing runs are not a robust improvement for a painting built around movement through terrain. Pace 3 also showed severe early movement collapse during screening, so neither lower reward becomes the default.

Compact presets improve some Chaser tag counts, but the asymmetric confirmation raises Runner falls to 3.60 per episode; seed 303 reaches 6.19. Compact Chaser landings also remain low. This does not justify replacing the current architecture or trying an unmeasured combination with pace 8.

## Verification and next experiments

Four generations of cleanup-only code exactly reproduced the original seed-202 populations, champions, archive and retained metadata. Four generations of the final code exactly reproduced the separate-validation intervention, with assertions that held-out validation leaves every breeding fitness unchanged. A pinned-source 12-generation replay also reproduced both original seed-202 populations. TypeScript, production build, deterministic episode replay and 720 focused descending-landing cases passed.

The next useful test is longer training of the corrected baseline, followed by a single controlled change that improves Chaser pursuit without sacrificing Runner movement. Keep the same common-panel comparisons, inspect individual seeds, and observe retained pairs in the visible arena. Lower pace reward and smaller networks should remain experimental until they survive that test. Avoid adding more shaping terms based on these short runs alone.

Three initialization seeds and 12–24 generations screen failure modes; they do not establish statistical significance, long-run convergence, or visual quality. High fall counts and low Chaser traversal remain unresolved learning problems. Physics/schema/reward formulas are unchanged by this selection fix, and no experimental checkpoint is installed as the user's display champion.
